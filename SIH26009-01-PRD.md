## 1. The official problem statement

> **Background:** MOIL Limited is the largest producer of Manganese Ore in India. To meet future demand, it is important to accurately identify available reserves and avoid production shortfalls. At present, reserve estimation and production planning are mainly based on manual surveys, drilling results, and production records. These methods are time-consuming and sometimes lead to a mismatch between expected and actual ore production.
>
> **Detailed Description:** The challenge is to develop an AI/ML-based solution that uses geological data, historical production, equipment performance, and satellite/space technology inputs (such as rainfall, soil moisture, vegetation index, and land temperature) to:
> - Identify and map manganese reserves more accurately using surface and sub-surface indicators.
> - Predict shortfalls in production by analysing constraints like equipment downtime, weather conditions, or blasting delays.
> - Suggest corrective actions such as adjusting mine schedules, optimizing blasting, or re-deploying equipment to ensure continuous ore availability.
>
> **Expected Solution:** The expected solution is a user-friendly dashboard that shows predicted reserves, production trends, possible risks of shortfall, and recommended corrective steps. This will help MOIL improve planning, reduce losses, and ensure steady ore supply to customers.

## 2. Interpretation — what this is actually asking, and the trap in it

### 2.1 This is two products, not one **[D]**

The statement bolts together two problems with different data, different methods, different users and different time horizons. Treating them as one system is the most common way to fail this PS.

| | **Track A — Reserve identification** | **Track B — Production shortfall** |
|---|---|---|
| Question | *Where is the ore, and how much?* | *Will we hit target this month, and what do we do if not?* |
| Data | Geology, drill logs, assays, surface mapping | Production records, equipment logs, blasting logs, weather |
| Method | Geostatistics + prospectivity classification | Time-series forecasting + constraint reasoning |
| Horizon | Years | Days to months |
| User | Exploration geologist, planning | Mine manager, production control |
| Satellite role | **Surface indicators only** | Environmental drivers — the listed inputs belong here |

### 2.2 The trap: you cannot see subsurface manganese from orbit **[D]**

Note precisely which satellite inputs the PS names: **rainfall, soil moisture, vegetation index, land surface temperature.** Every one of those is a *surface or atmospheric* signal. They describe weather and ground conditions. They do not penetrate rock.

MOIL's flagship Balaghat mine works at roughly 383 m depth. No optical, thermal or conventional SAR sensor images ore at that depth. A team claiming satellite-based subsurface reserve detection will be dismantled in one question by a MOIL geologist on the jury.

**Read the PS carefully and it does not actually ask for that.** It says "using surface **and** sub-surface indicators" — surface indicators come from remote sensing, sub-surface indicators come from drilling and geophysics. Those are two input families feeding one model, not one sensor doing both.

### 2.3 What satellite data legitimately contributes **[P]**

**To Track A (surface indicators — prospectivity, not detection):**
- Lithological and alteration mapping of surface exposures from multispectral band ratios — the Sausar Group gondite horizons that host MOIL's manganese are mapped at surface.
- Structural lineament extraction from DEM and SAR. These deposits are structurally controlled; fold hinges and shear zones are where the ore horizons thicken.
- Mine footprint, dump and stockpile change detection over time.
- **InSAR ground deformation** over century-old underground workings — subsidence monitoring. Not asked for, genuinely valuable, and a strong differentiator.

**To Track B (the inputs the PS actually names):**
- Rainfall and soil moisture → monsoon disruption, haul-road trafficability, pit inundation, dump instability.
- NDVI and LST → secondary context, dump revegetation, surface condition.

**How to say it to a jury:** *"We use satellite data for what it can actually measure. Surface geology and structure inform where to prospect; weather and ground conditions predict production disruption. We do not claim to see ore underground, because nobody can."*

### 2.4 Scope boundary: prospectivity, not statutory reserves **[D]**

In India, mineral resource and reserve reporting is governed by the **UNFC** framework and the **Minerals (Evidence of Mineral Contents) Rules, 2015**, with reporting to the **Indian Bureau of Mines**. A number labelled "reserve" carries legal and financial meaning.

**Our system must not output statutory reserve figures.** It outputs **prospectivity scores, resource-model updates and uncertainty ranges as decision support for a qualified person.** Stating this boundary explicitly is a credibility gain with a PSU jury, not a limitation.

## 3. Users

| Persona | Role | What they need | Track |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exploration geologist** | Decides where to drill next | Ranked prospective zones with confidence and the evidence behind each | A |
| **Mine manager** (per mine) | Owns monthly production target | Shortfall risk for the next N days, and what to do about it | B |
| **Production planning / HQ** | Consolidated view across ~10 mines | Portfolio dashboard, trend, variance vs. plan | B |
| **Equipment / maintenance lead** | HEMM availability | Downtime patterns, redeployment suggestions | B |
| **Marketing / dispatch** | Customer supply commitments | Forward ore availability by grade | B |

**Operating context [A — verify]:** MOIL runs roughly ten mines across Nagpur and Bhandara districts (Maharashtra) and Balaghat district (Madhya Pradesh); most are underground, a few opencast; Balaghat is the largest and deepest. Ore is sold in grades — high grade for ferro-manganese, medium for silico-manganese, blast-furnace grade, and dioxide for dry cells. **Grade matters: a shortfall in one grade is not fungible with a surplus in another.** Forecasting must be grade-aware.

## 4. Goals and non-goals

### Goals
1. **[PS]** Improve accuracy of manganese reserve identification and mapping.
2. **[PS]** Predict production shortfalls from equipment, weather and blasting constraints.
3. **[PS]** Recommend corrective actions — schedule adjustment, blasting optimisation, equipment redeployment.
4. **[PS]** Deliver all of the above through a user-friendly dashboard.
5. **[D]** Make every prediction traceable to its inputs, so a planning engineer can defend it.

### Non-goals
1. **[D]** Producing statutory UNFC-classified reserve figures. See §2.4.
2. **[P]** Real-time equipment control or SCADA integration.
3. **[P]** Replacing drilling. The system directs drilling; it does not substitute for it.
4. **[P]** Geophysical inversion from raw survey data.
5. **[P]** Financial or commodity price forecasting.
6. **[P]** Mine safety and ventilation management.

## 8. Data requirements — and the biggest risk in this project

### 8.1 Required inputs **[PS]**

Geological data · historical production · equipment performance · satellite inputs (rainfall, soil moisture, vegetation index, land temperature).

### 8.2 The problem

**MOIL's borehole logs, assay databases, block models, HEMM downtime records and blasting logs are proprietary and not publicly available.** No dataset was found attached to this PS.

This is the central feasibility constraint and it must be confronted in the design, not glossed over. Two consequences:

1. The system must be built against a **documented ingestion contract** — a published schema that MOIL can map its real data onto. The contract is a deliverable in its own right.
2. Development and demonstration run on **open surrogates plus synthetic data generated to realistic distributions**, clearly labelled as such.

**Say this to a jury rather than hiding it.** A team that says *"here is the exact schema MOIL plugs into, here is our synthetic generator calibrated to published production figures, and here is what changes when real data arrives"* is more credible than one implying it has data it does not.

### 8.3 Open and surrogate sources **[P]**

| Need | Source |
|---|---|
| Regional geology, borehole and geochemical baseline | GSI **Bhukosh** portal; GSI Sausar Group mapping |
| Mine-wise production, grade, reserves at national level | **IBM Indian Minerals Yearbook** — manganese chapter |
| MOIL production, grade mix, mine-level figures | MOIL annual reports and investor presentations |
| Optical multispectral | Sentinel-2 L2A; Resourcesat LISS-III/AWiFS via **Bhoonidhi** |
| Lithological band ratios | ASTER (SWIR bands support alteration mapping) |
| SAR / InSAR | Sentinel-1 GRD and SLC |
| DEM | **CartoDEM** (ISRO), SRTM, ALOS |
| Rainfall | IMD gridded; GPM IMERG; INSAT via **MOSDAC** |
| Soil moisture | SMAP; Sentinel-1 derived |
| NDVI / LST | Sentinel-2, MODIS, Landsat 8/9 TIRS |
| Basemap and thematic layers | **Bhuvan** |
| Equipment downtime, blasting logs | **No open source — synthetic, schema-documented** |

### 8.4 Data quality requirements **[D]**

- All spatial layers reprojected to a single working CRS; **area and tonnage computations in an equal-area or appropriate UTM projection, never in degrees.**
- Cloud masking mandatory on optical inputs; cloud fraction over the AOI recorded with every derived product.
- Temporal alignment: production is monthly or daily, weather is daily, satellite is per-revisit. Resampling policy must be explicit and recorded.
- Missing-data policy stated per field. Never impute silently.

## 10. MVP and phasing

### Phase 1 — internal screening (PPT + video)
Not a product. A thesis, an architecture, and one working vertical slice.
- One mine as the pilot AOI **[P]**
- **Track B end to end**: ingest surrogate production + real weather → forecast → shortfall probability with attribution → one corrective recommendation with its expected effect
- **Track A thin**: prospectivity surface from satellite surface indicators over the pilot AOI, with uncertainty, on a map
- Dashboard shell covering D-1 to D-4 and the evidence panel (D-7)
- The published **ingestion contract** for MOIL data
- One backtest number, honestly reported

**Not in Phase 1:** InSAR, scenario comparison, multi-mine portfolio, role-based views, export, alerting.

**Lead with Track B.** It is where the satellite inputs the PS names actually apply, it is demonstrable on obtainable data, and it is the half MOIL can act on next month.

### Phase 2 — offline internal hackathon
Multi-mine portfolio, grade-aware forecasting, scenario comparison, constraint engine hardened, InSAR subsidence overlay, export, alerting, full backtest table.

### Phase 3 — Grand Finale (36 hours, December 2026)
Depth not breadth: uncertainty calibration curves, recommendation accept/reject audit trail, on-premise deployment demonstration, and a written technical note plus the data contract suitable for handing to MOIL.

## 12. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **No access to MOIL operational data** | **Highest** | Ingestion contract + calibrated synthetic generator. Declare it openly. |
| **Overclaiming subsurface detection from satellite** | **Highest** | §2.2. Make the boundary an explicit slide before a juror asks. |
| Domain depth — mining geology and operations are unfamiliar | High | Scheduled study: Sausar Group geology, UNFC, HEMM operations, blasting cycles. Ground every assumption in IBM/MOIL published material. |
| Two-track scope is too large for the team | High | Track B is the spine. Track A ships thin in Phase 1 and deepens later. |
| Recommender proposes something operationally absurd | High | Hard constraint engine (C-5), not a learned policy. |
| Modelling skill gap — this needs tabular ML and time-series, not API orchestration | Medium | Mostly gradient boosting, classical geostatistics and forecasting. Far more accessible than deep learning research, but it *is* model training. Budget for it. |
| "Reserve" outputs read as statutory claims | Medium | §2.4 boundary stated in the UI itself, not just the deck. |
| Satellite inputs contribute little to Track A and the jury notices | Medium | Be specific about what each band ratio and lineament layer contributes; do not include layers for decoration. |
